The resignations you'll receive in January are being decided this month.

Few people quit in December. The bonus hasn't cleared, the holidays are coming, and starting a job search in the last two weeks of the year feels pointless. So people wait.

But waiting isn't the same as staying. December is when a tired employee does the quiet math: another year of this, or not. By the time the letter lands on your desk in January, the decision is weeks old.

That's what makes it expensive. Replacing one employee costs 6–9 months of their salary. (SHRM) And you rarely lose the person who was already looking. You lose the reliable one who stopped raising their hand in October and nobody noticed.

You can't stop every January resignation. Some of them are healthy. But you can find out who's at risk while there's still time to do something about it.

Three things worth doing before the break:

Look at who has stopped showing up to optional things.
Ask managers which of their people they'd least want to lose, then ask when they last had a real conversation with them.
Give people one concrete thing to look forward to in Q1 that isn't another all-hands.

None of it costs much. All of it is cheaper than a backfill.

Who on your team would you least want to see resign in January?
