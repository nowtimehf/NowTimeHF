"We tried this. Nobody had the time."

I hear this more than any other objection, and it's the one I take most seriously — because it's usually true.

Here's what it actually means: the program asked people to find time they don't have.

Ninety minutes. A commute to a gym. A lunch hour surrendered to a webinar. A meditation app that assumes twenty quiet minutes exist in a workday that has none.

Your employees didn't fail that program. That program was designed for someone with a calendar they don't have.

So the design question isn't "how do we motivate them?" It's "what fits in the gaps that actually exist?"

For us that means twenty-minute programming. Desk-side mindfulness under three minutes. Nutrition guidance that survives a ten-hour day and a client dinner.

Not because short is trendy. Because short is the only thing that survives contact with a real workweek.

If your last program died of time, the fix isn't more encouragement. It's a smaller ask.
