"We're waiting on our engagement survey results."

Fair. It's a reasonable instinct to want data before spending money. I'd rather work with an HR leader who asks for data than one who doesn't.

But look at what a survey can and can't tell you.

It tells you how people said they felt on the day they answered. It's anonymous, it's annual or twice a year, and the people who are most burned out are often the least likely to fill it in honestly, or at all.

What it can't tell you is who will actually use something if you offer it. That's a different question, and it's the one that decides whether your wellness spend works.

The industry average for wellness program participation is 6–8%. A survey score of "moderately stressed" doesn't move that number. Usage data does, because it shows you who started, who stopped, and when.

So run both. Read the survey for sentiment. Then run something small and measure behavior: who came to the session, who opened the platform in week two, who was still there in week four.

That's what the Pilot Sprint report is. Not how people feel about wellness. What they actually did with it.
