"You're a one-person company."

Yes. That's the product.

It's a fair thing to raise, so let me answer it straight rather than dance around it.

When you hire a large wellness vendor, you get a platform, an implementation team, and an account manager who is responsible for eleven other accounts. When engagement drops in month three, it shows up in a quarterly review, if it shows up at all.

When you hire me, engagement dropping in month three is my problem that week. There's no queue and nobody to escalate to. I see the numbers, I chase the people who went quiet, and I answer for it directly.

That's a real trade-off and I won't pretend otherwise: I can't onboard four thousand employees next Monday. If you need that, hire someone who can.

But for a 50–200 person company, "one accountable human" isn't a limitation. It's the whole thing you couldn't buy from the enterprise vendor at any price.

I'm a service-disabled veteran-owned small business. My name is on this. I don't get to burn clients and move on to the next logo.

Small enough to answer the phone. Structured enough to prove it worked.
