Write your January wellness email before you leave for the break. Not after.

Here's why. The first week back is the one window of the year when people are actually open to starting something. By week three, the inbox is full and the old routine is back.

If the email goes out on January 12 because that's when you got around to writing it, you've missed the window.

So write it now, schedule it for the first Monday back, and keep it short. Three things:

One ask. Not "explore our wellness resources." Something specific: "Join a 20-minute session on Thursday at noon."

One date. A real one, close enough to commit to. Not "sometime in Q1."

One name. A person people can reply to. Messages from a named human get answered. Messages from "the Benefits Team" get archived.

That's it. No list of twelve programs. No link to a portal with a login they forgot in March.

The companies with the best January participation don't have better programs. They just asked clearly, on day one, before anyone had time to fall back into the old pattern.

What's the one thing you'd ask your team to do in their first week back?
