The holiday party is not a wellness strategy.

I'm not against the party. Go. Have a good time. Thank your people.

But a lot of companies quietly count it as the year's big morale investment, along with the summer outing and the pizza on launch day. One-off events, high cost per hour, and nothing that carries into the following Monday.

Here's the test I'd apply to anything in your culture or wellness budget: what is different two weeks later?

After the party: nothing, usually. Which is fine for a party. It isn't fine for the line item that's supposed to address burnout.

The things that actually change behavior look boring next to an open bar. They're small, they repeat, and somebody follows up.

A ten-minute habit people can do at their desk.
A session where attendance is tracked, so you know who didn't come.
A check-in in week two, because week two is when most people drift.

The party is a moment. A program is a rhythm. You need the moment too, just don't confuse it with the rhythm.

If you split your culture budget into "moments" and "rhythm," which side is bigger?
