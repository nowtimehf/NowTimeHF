Average participation in corporate wellness programs: 6–8%.

You're paying for 100%.

Run that against any other line item and see how it sounds. Software licensed for the whole company that 8% log into. A training program 8% complete. Office space 8% of people use.

Any of those would trigger a review. Wellness doesn't, because it's filed under "the right thing to do" rather than under things that are supposed to work.

I think that framing has done more damage to this category than any bad vendor. It lowers the bar to intention. And once the bar is intention, nobody checks the number.

Here's the more useful frame: employee wellness is an operational program with a measurable adoption rate, and if adoption is 8%, the program has failed regardless of how good the underlying content is.

That's not cynicism about wellness. It's taking it seriously enough to hold it to the standard you'd hold anything else you bought.

What's your actual utilization? Not enrollment — usage. If nobody can answer that in one number, that's the finding.
