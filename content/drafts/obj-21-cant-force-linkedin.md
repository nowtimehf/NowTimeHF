"We can't force people to participate."

Agreed. You shouldn't.

But the Marine Corps taught me there's a big gap between forcing people and leaving it to chance.

PT wasn't left to how anyone felt that morning. There was a set time, a formation, and someone who noticed who was missing. The structure did the work, not motivation.

Most corporate wellness is built the opposite way. Optional, unscheduled, anonymous. Nobody counting, nobody noticing.

Then we're surprised when 6–8% of people use it.

I'm not saying run your office like boot camp. Please don't. Borrow the structure:

A set time on the calendar.
A small, repeatable task.
One person whose job is to notice who's missing.

You don't force it. You schedule it.

Who on your team is responsible for noticing when someone disappears?
