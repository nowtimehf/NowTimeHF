Open enrollment is the one month a year your entire workforce actually reads a benefits document.

Then they don't think about it again until next October.

That's the design flaw hiding inside almost every benefits package: the moment of maximum attention is followed by eleven months of silence. You buy for the enrollment window, but the problem you're buying against runs all year.

Ask it this way instead: what in this package will someone use in March?

Not enroll in. Use.

Most of the list won't survive that question. The gym stipend won't. The app nobody opens won't. The EAP hotline is designed not to be used until something breaks.

I'm not saying cut them. I'm saying know which line items are insurance and which ones are supposed to change behavior — because they're being budgeted as if they're the same thing, and they aren't.

The ones meant to change behavior need a March plan. Otherwise you're buying a very expensive October.

What's on your list this year that someone will still be using in the spring?
