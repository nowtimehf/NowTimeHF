Nobody has ever asked me what the name means.

Now Time Health + Fitness. Both words are an acronym.

NOW: No Other Way.
TIME: Time Is My Everything.

I built it as a workout program years before it was a company. The whole idea was that there is no time to waste — so use what you have, and use it well.

I didn't think of that as positioning. It was just true.

Then I started selling to HR leaders, and I kept hearing the same objection. Not from them. From their employees.

"I don't have time."

That objection is most of the reason the industry average for corporate wellness participation sits at 6–8%. Not apathy. Not laziness. Time.

Every failed program I've watched asked a burned-out person to find 90 spare minutes, drive somewhere, and figure it out alone. Of course they didn't go.

So we built the opposite. Programming that fits inside a lunch break. Mindfulness you can run at your desk in under three minutes. Nutrition that survives a ten-hour workday.

I learned that in the Marine Corps, where nobody waits for a convenient moment. Then I spent 15 years on the gym floor watching the same lesson repeat: the people who change aren't the ones with the most time. They're the ones with the smallest non-negotiables.

No other way. Time is my everything.

It took me longer than I'd like to admit to notice the name had been describing the solution the whole time.

If your wellness program is fighting the clock instead of designing around it, that's the thing to fix first.
