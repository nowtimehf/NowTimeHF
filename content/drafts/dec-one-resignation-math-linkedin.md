Do the math on one resignation before you decide wellness doesn't fit next year's budget.

Replacing one employee costs 6–9 months of their salary. (SHRM)

Take a mid-level employee at $70,000.

Six months: $35,000.
Nine months: $52,500.

That's one person. Not a team, not a department. One resignation from someone who was quietly running on empty.

Now put that next to the Pilot Sprint: $1,500–$2,500, one time. One live workshop, 30 days of platform access for everyone who attended, and an engagement report at the end.

I'm not going to tell you a workshop prevents a resignation. I can't prove that, and you shouldn't trust anyone who says they can.

What I can tell you is what the report shows: who engaged, who didn't, and who dropped off in week two. The people who drop off are often the same people you'd least want to lose, and right now you have no way of seeing them.

So the question isn't "can we afford a wellness program?" It's "what does it cost us to keep not knowing?"

You can check this against your own numbers in about five minutes: your average salary, your turnover last year, your backfill time.
