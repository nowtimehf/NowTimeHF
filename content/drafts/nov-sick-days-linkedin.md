Burnout shows up on your insurance renewal before it shows up in a survey.

Burned-out employees are 63% more likely to take a sick day and 23% more likely to visit the ER. (Gallup)

Read that second number again with your benefits renewal open.

Sick days are visible. You can pull them in a report. ER visits aren't — they surface a year later as claims experience, folded into a premium increase that nobody connects back to the team that was running on empty last spring.

That's why burnout keeps getting filed under culture. Its costs arrive in a different department, on a different calendar, under a different name.

I'm not a medical provider, and I won't claim a wellness program lowers anyone's claims on its own. What it can do is catch the slide earlier — when someone quietly stops showing up to the things they used to, which tends to happen well before the sick days start.

If you're looking at a renewal this quarter, put two lists side by side: where your sick days concentrate, and who's gone quiet. Check whether they're the same names.
