"Our people won't share health data."

Good. They shouldn't have to, and I'd be worried about a vendor who wanted it.

This objection comes up more since every wellness platform started marketing biometric screening, and it deserves a straight answer rather than a reassurance.

I don't collect diagnoses. I don't want your employees' medical history, their bloodwork, or anything that would sit near HIPAA. That isn't a limitation I'm working around — it's the design.

What I measure is behavior, in aggregate:

Who is active weekly. What content gets used and what gets ignored. Where completion drops off. Self-reported energy and stress, voluntarily, against their own baseline.

You get participation by department and cohort. You never get an individual's health status, because I never have it.

That distinction matters more than most vendors admit. A program that needs personal medical data to prove value has built its reporting on the one thing employees are most right to protect — and the moment they sense that, participation falls.

You can measure whether a program works without knowing anyone's cholesterol.
