People ask what actually happens in the 60-minute workshop. Here's the honest answer, including what doesn't happen.

What doesn't happen: a lecture on sleep hygiene with a slide of a person meditating on a beach. A fitness test. Anyone being asked to share anything personal in front of their boss.

What does happen:

Your team picks one of three: Battle-Tested Resilience, The Executive Athlete, or Mindfulness at Work.

I run it live. Not a video, not a facilitator reading my notes. The room is the point. It's the one time everyone is in the same place, trying the same thing, at the same moment.

Everyone leaves with something they can use that afternoon. A technique that fits between meetings, not a 90-minute routine nobody will do.

And then the part most workshops skip: everyone who attended gets platform access for the next 30 days, and I watch the numbers. Who logged in. Who came back in week two. Who drifted.

That's the difference. A one-off workshop is an event. A workshop with a 30-day follow-up and a report is the start of a program.

The resilience session borrows from what I learned in the Marine Corps about performing under pressure. Not the yelling. The structure.
