Replacing one employee costs 6–9 months of their salary. (SHRM)

Sit with what that means for a mid-size company.

One director-level departure — someone at $110k, say — costs somewhere between $55,000 and $82,000 once you count recruitment, ramp time, the productivity dip across their team, and the institutional knowledge that walks out with them.

That's one person. Most companies I talk to lost three or four this year they'd describe, privately, as avoidable.

Now compare that to what an entire wellness program costs.

I'm not going to claim wellness prevents every departure, because it doesn't. Plenty of people leave for money, or a title, or a genuinely better job. Nothing I do touches that.

But the ones who left because they were ground down over eighteen months and nobody noticed until the resignation letter? That's the population this is for. And you already know their names.

The math doesn't need to be aggressive to work. Preventing one avoidable departure a year covers the program.

That's the whole business case. It fits in a sentence, and your CFO can check it.
