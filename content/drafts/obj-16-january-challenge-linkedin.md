"We'll launch a step challenge in January."

You can. Here's what usually happens.

Week one, the leaderboard fills up. The names at the top are the people who already run, already track their steps, already would have done it anyway. They're great. They weren't your problem.

Week three, the board goes quiet. Week six, it's three people and an automated reminder.

That isn't a failure of effort. It's a selection effect. A voluntary challenge recruits whoever was already motivated, which is a big part of why the industry average sits at 6–8% participation.

The people you're actually worried about — the overloaded manager, the high performer who's stopped volunteering for things — don't join leaderboards. A public ranking is the last place a struggling person wants to appear.

So by all means run something in January. Just change what you measure. Not who won. Who never started, and who stopped in week two.

That list is the program. The leaderboard is decoration.
