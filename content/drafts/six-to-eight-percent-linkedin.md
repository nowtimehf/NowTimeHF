Most corporate wellness programs land single-digit participation. Industry benchmarks put the average around 6–8%.

Every vendor knows this. Very few lead with it.

Here's why it matters more than any feature comparison: at 7% participation, you are not running a wellness program. You are subsidizing the habits of the people who were already healthy.

The employees driving your absenteeism and turnover costs — the burned-out, overloaded, quietly disengaging ones — are almost definitionally not in that 7%.

So the program that looks like it's working is often working hardest for the people who needed it least.

That's not an employee motivation problem. It's a selection problem baked into the design. Voluntary access, no structure, no follow-up: you will always recruit the already-converted.

The fix isn't a better app. It's a program that starts with a live session everyone attends, then follows up with the people who go quiet.

Access self-selects. Accountability doesn't.
