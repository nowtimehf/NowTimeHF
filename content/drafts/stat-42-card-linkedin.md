42% of burned-out employees tell their manager. (Mind Share Partners)

You meet the other 58% at their exit interview.

That's where the real information lives in most companies — in a conversation held after the decision is final, with someone who has no reason left to soften it.

Every HR leader I know has had that meeting. Sat across from someone genuinely good, heard exactly what went wrong, and recognized that all of it was fixable eighteen months earlier.

The exit interview is an excellent diagnostic and a terrible intervention. It only runs on people you've already lost.

So the question worth asking this quarter isn't whether your people would tell you. It's what you're measuring that doesn't depend on them telling you.

Participation is one. Someone who quietly stops showing up to optional things is giving you the same information the exit interview would — six months earlier, and while you can still do something with it.

The data you need isn't confidential. It's just not being collected.
