"Leadership won't sign off on this."

Leadership doesn't buy wellness. They buy the report.

I've watched good HR directors lose this argument for years, and it's almost never because the executive team doesn't care about employees. It's because wellness arrives in the budget meeting as a feeling and everything else arrives as a number.

You cannot win that room with "our people are tired."

You can win it with: here's what turnover cost us last year, here's the utilization rate on what we currently pay for, and here's a 90-day test that produces a real participation number for less than we spend on the holiday party.

That's not a wellness pitch. That's a pilot with a defined cost and a measurable output — which is a language every executive already speaks.

This is also why I hand over an engagement report instead of a testimonial. Not because stories don't matter, but because you need something you can put in a deck.

Your CFO isn't the obstacle. The absence of a number is.
