"We already have an EAP."

Good. Keep it. I'm not trying to replace it, and any vendor who tells you otherwise doesn't understand what an EAP is for.

But notice what it's built to do.

An Employee Assistance Program is a door someone walks through when things have already gone wrong. It's reactive by design, confidential by necessity, and — for exactly those reasons — almost impossible to measure.

Typical EAP utilization runs in the low single digits. That's not a failure. Crisis support should be rarely needed.

The problem is when it's the only thing in the budget.

Because the employee who's exhausted but functional, sleeping badly, skipping lunch, quietly drafting a resignation — that person is not calling the EAP hotline. They don't think they qualify. And they're right, until suddenly they do.

That's the population a daily-structure program is for. Not crisis. Drift.

An EAP catches people who fall. Our job is to notice who's tilting.

Both should exist. Only one of them tells you what's happening before it happens.
