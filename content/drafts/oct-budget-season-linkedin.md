It's budget season. Here's how wellness usually dies in it.

Someone asks what the return is. The honest answer is "we think people feel better." That's a real outcome and a terrible budget line, and it loses to anything with a number attached.

So bring a number.

Replacing one employee costs 6–9 months of their salary. (SHRM) You don't need a study to apply that — you know what you paid to backfill roles this year, and you know which of those departures were about workload rather than opportunity.

That figure is your denominator. Wellness spend that prevents one avoidable departure has paid for itself, and everyone in the room can check the math themselves.

Then the second number: your current benefit's actual utilization. If it's single digits, you're not asking for new money — you're asking to move existing spend to something measurable. That's a much easier conversation, and it's usually true.

Budget arguments aren't won on need. They're won by whoever shows up with arithmetic the room can verify.
