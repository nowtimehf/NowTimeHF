"We're fully remote. This won't work."

Remote teams are the reason it's built the way it is.

Think about what a distributed company actually lost. Not the office — the incidental structure. The walk to the train. The lunch someone dragged you to. The colleague who noticed you looked wrecked on Tuesday.

None of that was a wellness program. All of it was doing the work of one.

What replaced it, for most remote employees, is a chair, a screen, and back-to-back calls with no natural edges. No commute means no transition. No hallway means nobody notices.

So an on-site gym benefit isn't just useless to that person. It's slightly insulting.

What works instead is the thing that travels: programming on a phone, sessions short enough to fit between meetings, a live workshop everyone joins from wherever they are, and check-ins that don't depend on physical proximity to happen.

Distributed teams don't need less structure. They need structure that isn't tied to a building.

If your wellness benefit assumes a commute, it was designed for a company you no longer are.
