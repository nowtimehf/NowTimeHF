Your wellness program runs through your managers whether you designed it that way or not.

An employee doesn't experience "the benefits package." They experience whether their manager noticed they've been off for three weeks, and what happened when they mentioned it.

That's where most programs quietly fail. The platform gets bought at the company level. The noticing is left to people who were never told it's part of the job, never shown what to look for, and never given anything to offer once they do notice.

So a struggling employee gets a sympathetic manager and nowhere to send them. Which, from the outside, looks exactly like a manager who doesn't care.

Three things fix most of it, and none cost much:

Tell managers that noticing is part of the role.
Give them two or three concrete things they can actually offer.
Show them the participation data for their team, so the quiet ones are visible.

The program is the infrastructure. Managers are the delivery.

What does a manager at your company actually do today when someone says they're struggling?
