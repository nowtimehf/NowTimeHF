The last week before a long break is when a lot of people quietly decide whether they're coming back.

Not dramatically. Nobody announces it. They just finish the year, log off, and use the time away to think.

Only 42% of burned-out employees tell their manager. And 42% of those say nothing changed after they did. (Mind Share Partners)

So the majority of your burned-out people are going into the holidays having told no one, and some of the ones who did speak up are going in feeling ignored.

There's one cheap thing a manager can do this week. A five-minute conversation with each direct report that isn't about deliverables. Something like:

"Before we break, how are you actually doing this year? Not the project. You."

Then the important part: don't fix anything in the moment. Just listen, and write down one thing to follow up on in January. Then actually follow up.

That's not therapy, and managers shouldn't pretend it is. It's noticing. Most people don't need a solution in December. They need evidence that someone saw them.

If your managers did one thing this week, what would you want it to be?
