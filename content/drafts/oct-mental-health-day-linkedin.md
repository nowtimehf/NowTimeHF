World Mental Health Day is this Saturday. Most companies will post something.

Here's how to tell whether yours means it: look at what happens on Monday.

The post is easy. A graphic, a line about how it's okay not to be okay, comments from the leadership team. None of it costs anything, and none of it is untrue.

But the employee reading it already knows the company can produce a graphic. What they're actually assessing is whether raising their hand on Monday changes anything — and they've usually got evidence either way.

Only 42% of burned-out employees ever tell their manager. Of those who do, 42% say nothing changed. (Mind Share Partners)

That second number is the one that kills a wellness culture. It's not silence that does the damage, it's speaking up and watching it go nowhere.

So before scheduling the post, ask what a person gets if they take it seriously. A named next step? A manager trained to respond? Anything at all beyond a sympathetic reply?

If the honest answer is "nothing specific" — fix that first, post second. The graphic will still be there next year.
