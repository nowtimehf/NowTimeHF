76% of employees experience burnout at work at least sometimes. 28% feel it very often or always. (Gallup)

Most HR leaders know a number like that. Fewer have connected it to the one their CFO cares about.

Replacing a single employee costs 6–9 months of their salary. (SHRM)

Run that on your own headcount. Not the whole company — just the people you'd genuinely struggle to replace. Then ask what your current wellness spend is doing to protect them.

For most of the companies I talk to, the honest answer is: nothing measurable. There's a stipend on the books. Utilization is unknown. Nobody has checked in eighteen months.

That's not a wellness problem. That's an unmanaged line item with turnover attached to it.

The gap between "we offer wellness" and "our people use it" is where the money goes. And it's the only part of this you can actually control.

Do you know your wellness benefit's real utilization rate? Not enrollment — usage.

If the answer is "not really," that's the first number worth finding.
