"Our turnover isn't that bad."

Maybe not. But turnover is the last symptom, not the first.

By the time someone resigns, the decision is typically months old. What you're measuring in your turnover number is a problem that started long before, went unaddressed, and finally resolved itself in the way you least wanted.

Low turnover can mean your culture is healthy. It can also mean a soft job market, or people who are stuck rather than staying.

The earlier signals look nothing like resignations:

Your best people have stopped volunteering for things. Nobody pushes back in meetings anymore. Sick days cluster around the same handful of names. The wellness benefit you pay for has 6% utilization and nobody's asked why.

None of that shows up in a turnover report. All of it precedes one.

So "our turnover isn't that bad" and "our people are fine" are different claims, and only one of them is on a dashboard.

If you want to know which you've got, look at engagement with anything voluntary. That number moves first.
