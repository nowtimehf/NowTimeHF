Participation in almost everything drops in December. Plan for it now, or explain it in January.

Holidays, travel, year-end deadlines, school events. Your people's calendars fill with things that are genuinely more important than a wellness app, and they should.

The mistake is treating the December dip as failure. It isn't. It's predictable, and anything predictable can be designed for.

What that looks like in practice:

Shrink the ask. Ten-minute sessions instead of thirty. One habit instead of five.
Name it out loud. "December is maintenance month" gives people permission to do less without quitting entirely.
Watch who returns in January, not who dropped in December. Coming back is the real signal.

The programs that hold through the holidays aren't the ones that push harder in December. They're the ones that lowered the bar on purpose and kept the thread intact.

If your program has no December plan, it has a January restart problem.

What does your team's version of a "maintenance month" look like?
