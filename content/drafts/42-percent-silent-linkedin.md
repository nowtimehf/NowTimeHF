Only 42% of burned-out employees ever tell their manager. And of those who do, 42% say nothing changed. (Mind Share Partners)

Sit with that second number for a second.

Nearly half the people brave enough to raise their hand watched it go nowhere. Those people don't raise their hand twice. They update their resume instead.

This is why "our people would tell us if there was a problem" is the most expensive assumption in HR.

They mostly don't. And when they do, the system frequently doesn't respond — not out of malice, but because nobody owned the follow-up.

Which is the actual design flaw. Not awareness. Not benefits. Follow-through.

Here's a test worth running this week: pick your last five wellness or workload concerns raised by employees. Can you name what happened to each one?

If you can't, your people already know that. It's why they've stopped telling you.

The fix isn't another survey. It's assigning someone to own what happens after the survey.
