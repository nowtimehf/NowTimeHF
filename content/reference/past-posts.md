## November 21 — 90-Second Rule for Managing Stress

Graphic: https://drive.google.com/file/d/10y7WOk-cGP6EODdm5BSFSjw9k8y5FAVc/view?usp=sharing

Stressful moments at work are always unavoidable.

There's always a tense message, a sudden request, an unexpected change. Often, one small trigger changes your energy for the entire day.

What you can do for managing this is the 90-Second Rule.

Research shows that when something stressful happens, the body releases stress chemicals that peak for about 90 seconds. After that, the physical reaction naturally settles… unless the mind keeps replaying the situation.

Try this when something stressful hits at your workplace:

1\. Pause for 90 seconds. 2\. Focus on breathing instead of reacting. 3\. Let the surge move through your system. 4\. Then respond from clarity, not from urgency.

It gives your nervous system time to catch up so the response aligns with the intention.

Sometimes the most valuable thing you can give yourself at work is a brief pause.

## December 5 — Underrated Habit for Better Focus

Graphic: https://drive.google.com/file/d/11EsHLy9laTB3Rlg8chPyXou6I7xTfDKf/view?usp=sharing

One of the most underrated habits for better focus is choosing to do one thing fully before moving on to the next.

Most people don’t realize how much attention they lose when they switch tasks without pausing.

The brain carries leftover thoughts, emotions, and tension from the previous task into the next one, which is why people often have trouble concentrating even when they’re trying to be fully present.

A transition pause interrupts that mental residue. Taking a slow breath, straightening your posture, or simply acknowledging, “I’m shifting now,” gives your nervous system the signal to reset.

That tiny reset clears cognitive clutter and makes it easier for your focus to lock onto what’s in front of you.

Focus isn’t just about discipline. It’s about resetting your mind so it’s actually able to pay attention.
