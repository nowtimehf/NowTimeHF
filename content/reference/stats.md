# Stat Library

The brand's whole differentiator is *"I don't sell engagement promises. I sell
engagement reporting."* A loose number in a post undoes that in one line.

**Rule: only use a stat from the Approved table below, and always name the
source in the post.** If a number isn't here, don't post it.

## ✅ Approved — sourced

| Stat | Source | Best used for |
|---|---|---|
| 76% of employees experience burnout at work at least sometimes; 28% very often or always | Gallup | Opening a cost-of-inaction post. The strongest hook in the library. |
| Replacing one employee costs **6–9 months of their salary** (50–200% depending on role) | SHRM | The money argument. Converts burnout into a CFO-legible number. |
| Only 42% of burned-out employees tell their manager — and 42% of those say nothing changed | Mind Share Partners (2025) | The "you can't see it" argument. Pairs with the engagement-reporting pitch. |
| Workplace stress costs U.S. employers **$300B+ annually** in absenteeism, diminished productivity, and turnover | The American Institute of Stress | Macro framing. Widely cited but very broad — see caution below. |
| Burned-out employees are **63% more likely** to take a sick day and **23% more likely** to visit the ER | (attributed in the capability statement; originally Gallup) | Linking burnout directly to insurance cost. |
| Average corporate wellness program participation: **6–8%** | industry-wide, as cited in the capability statement | The engagement-gap argument. The core of the whole pitch. |

## ⛔ Do not use

| Figure | Why |
|---|---|
| **"$1.75+ per employee per month in avoidable healthcare and absenteeism costs"** | Works out to ~$21/employee/year — trivially small, and it argues *against* buying anything. Contradicts the brand's own $300B framing (≈$1,800/employee/year). Almost certainly a lost order of magnitude. Appears in the Cost-of-Inaction cold openers; **fix those before reuse.** |
| **"a real coach in every employee's pocket for about $50/month"** | A per-employee price that reconciles with nothing else in the material and undercuts the Pilot Sprint's $1,500–$2,500 flat fee. Don't quote a price publicly until it's confirmed. |

## ⚠️ Use with care

- **The $300B figure** is an old and very broad estimate. It's fine as framing,
  but it's the weakest number in the library for a skeptical HR director —
  it's abstract and every competitor cites it. **The SHRM 6–9 months of salary
  figure is far more persuasive** because the reader can compute their own
  number from it.
- **Any per-headcount extrapolation** (e.g. "$1,800/employee/year" derived by
  dividing $300B across the workforce) is arithmetic, not a published stat. If
  used, show the division openly and call it an average. A CFO will do this math
  themselves and catch an unstated assumption.

## Preferred framing

Lead with **76%** (the scale of the problem), land on **6–9 months of salary**
(the cost of ignoring it), and close on **6–8% participation** (why the current
spend isn't fixing it). That sequence moves from felt experience → money →
the gap NowTime fills, and every number in it is sourced.
